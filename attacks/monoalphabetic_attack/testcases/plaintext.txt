The one-time pad is popularly credited to Vernam [172], who filed a patent on it, but recent historical research [25] shows that it was invented some

35 years earlier. Analysis of the one-time pad had to await the ground-breaking work of Shannon [154], who introduced the notion of perfect secrecy.

In this chapter we studied perfectly secret encryption. Some other cryp-tographic problems can also be solved with "perfect" security. A notable example is the problem of message authentication where the aim is to prevent an adversary from (undetectably) modifying a message sent from one party to another. We study this problem in depth in Chapter 4, discussing "perfectly secure" message authentication in Section 4.6.
